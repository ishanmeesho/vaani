Sessions are ordered per user by the timestamp of their first turn — S1 is each user's very first Vaani session, S2 their second, and so on. Each bar shows the FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS split (User Turn Classifier L1) of all turns spoken in that session position; the line overlays the share of turns where a search was actually rendered (Vaani Turn Classifier L1 = search_happened). Covers all 167,082 users in the tagged dataset.
Turn mix by session position — FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS (100% stacked) + search-happened rate
What changed: The L1 mix is stable but drifts in one clear direction: FIND holds ~58–59% at every position, DECIDE grows from 27.3% in S1 to ~32% by S10, and OTHERS shrinks from 13.7% to ~9.7%. Search-happened rate barely moves (42.7% in S1 → ~42–43% throughout). Net: returning users don't search more — they ask more purchase-decision questions and produce less noise.
How many sessions exist at each position (funnel / retention)
What changed: Steep early drop-off: only 52% of the 167k S1 users come back for S2, 33% reach S3, and under 6% reach S10. Every later-session distribution therefore describes an increasingly self-selected, more engaged subset.

L1 + L2 evolution session-over-session

Each chart drills one L1 branch down to its L2 mix: the bars show what share of that branch's turns each L2 takes at every session position. Hover any segment for absolute turn counts.
FIND → L2 mix by session position (% of FIND turns)
What changed: Essentially flat — category_only (~43%) and category_with_attributes (~38–39%) dominate at every position. The only drift: attributes_only rises slightly (9.0% → 10.7%) while no_category falls (9.5% → 8.3%), i.e. experienced users refine on-screen results a bit more and make vague no-category asks a bit less.
DECIDE → L2 mix by session position (% of DECIDE turns)
What changed: The clearest evolution in the data: price grows from 60.0% of DECIDE turns in S1 to 66.5% by S10, with the loss coming almost entirely from quality (13.0% → 8.2%), plus smaller declines in delivery_time (5.6% → 4.5%) and how_to_use (2.3% → 1.5%). Repeat users increasingly use Vaani as a bargaining/price tool rather than a quality-vetting tool.
OTHERS → L2 mix by session position (% of OTHERS turns)
What changed: As OTHERS shrinks overall, its internal mix gets noisier: unresolved rises from 54.9% to ~58% of the branch, while off_journey and action_led_ask fade. Intentional off-topic and how-do-I-do-X asks disappear with experience; what remains of OTHERS in later sessions is mostly ASR gibberish.

What changed: Power users start different and converge to the overall pattern: S1 is unusually DECIDE-heavy (34.9% vs 27.3% overall) and FIND-light (53.1%), and the search-happened rate starts low at 36.6% — but session over session FIND climbs to ~58% and the search rate rises steadily to ~42% by S6–S10. L1 + L2 evolution session-over-session

Each chart drills one L1 branch down to its L2 mix: the bars show what share of that branch's turns each L2 takes at every session position. Hover any segment for absolute turn counts.
FIND → L2 mix by session position (% of FIND turns)
What changed: Power users begin with vaguer asks and sharpen them: no_category drops from 11.4% of FIND in S1 to 8.6% by S10, while category_only (40.5% → 42.1%) and category_with_attributes (37.1% → 38.5%) both rise. attributes_only stays ~11%. By S10 the FIND mix is indistinguishable from the overall population's.
DECIDE → L2 mix by session position (% of DECIDE turns)
What changed: Unlike the overall population, power users are price-obsessed from day one: price is already 64.6% of DECIDE in S1 and only inches up to 65.8% by S10 — the learning curve the general population shows over 10 sessions, power users have effectively completed before S1. The residual movement mirrors the overall trend: quality falls (11.7% → 8.4%) while other_product_information ticks up (10.8% → 12.3%).
OTHERS → L2 mix by session position (% of OTHERS turns)
What changed: Same direction as overall but starting cleaner: unresolved grows from 52.3% of OTHERS in S1 to ~58% by S10 as off_journey and action_led_ask decay. Combined with the shrinking OTHERS branch itself, genuine off-topic behaviour nearly vanishes for experienced power users — residual OTHERS is mostly unintelligible ASR.

Same session ordering as the other trend tabs (S1 = each user's first session). First chart: which app screen users were on when they spoke to Vaani, by session position. Then one chart per screen: how the FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS ask-mix on that screen evolves session-over-session. Covers all 167,082 users in the tagged dataset.
Where do turns happen — screen share by session position (100% stacked)
What changed: Turn volume consolidates onto two surfaces: SINGLE_PRODUCT grows from 46.0% of S1 turns to 51.9% by S10 and MAIN grows from 8.8% to 15.2%, while every checkout-side screen shrinks (CART 4.6% → 1.7%, PAYMENT_SELECTION 2.4% → 1.3%) and VOICE_ASSISTANT_INTRO_SCREEN disappears entirely (2.8% → 0%) — it's only shown to fresh users. Experienced users talk to Vaani mostly while inspecting a product or launching from the homepage.

Per-screen ask mix, session-over-session

SINGLE_PRODUCT — FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS mix by session position (% of turns on this screen)
What changed: The product page turns into a bargaining desk: DECIDE grows from 47.1% of turns in S1 to 53.3% by S10 while FIND drops 40.1% → 37.5% and OTHERS falls 12.7% → 9.2%. Repeat users on a product page increasingly interrogate price/size/quality instead of asking to see something else.
CATALOG_SEARCH_RESULTS — FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS mix by session position (% of turns on this screen)
What changed: Already FIND-dominated and gets more so: 86.2% → 88.3% FIND, with OTHERS shrinking 7.7% → 5.8%. On search results, users of every tenure do one thing — refine and re-search; that behaviour only sharpens.
MAIN — FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS mix by session position (% of turns on this screen)
What changed: Stable launcher behaviour: FIND holds 77.7% → 79.6% and DECIDE stays ~6%. OTHERS dips modestly (16.5% → 14.1%) — the homepage remains the place where sessions start with a fresh search ask, regardless of experience.
CART — FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS mix by session position (% of turns on this screen)
What changed: The biggest OTHERS cleanup of any screen: OTHERS falls 36.6% → 29.0% while DECIDE rises 46.2% → 52.3%. New users are confused/noisy at the cart; experienced ones ask pointed price/quantity/delivery questions before committing.
CATALOG_LISTING_PAGE — FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS mix by session position (% of turns on this screen)
What changed: Mirrors search results: FIND creeps 86.7% → 88.5%, OTHERS drops 10.2% → 7.5%. Listing pages are used the same way at every tenure — browse and refine.
VOICE_ASSISTANT_INTRO_SCREEN — FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS mix by session position (% of turns on this screen)
What changed: Mostly an S1–S2 phenomenon (it onboards new users) and volume goes to zero by S10. While it exists, its OTHERS share drops sharply (25.6% in S1 → ~14% for the few later-session turns) — first-touch confusion concentrated on first-touch users.
PAYMENT_SELECTION — FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS mix by session position (% of turns on this screen)
What changed: The noisiest screen at every tenure, but improving: OTHERS falls 48.0% → 41.4% as DECIDE rises 37.1% → 41.7%. Even by S10 nearly half the asks here don't map to a clean shopping intent — payment is where users most often say things Vaani can't act on.

MAIN — FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS mix by session position (% of turns on this screen)

CART — FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS mix by session position (% of turns on this screen)

CATALOG_LISTING_PAGE — FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS mix by session position (% of turns on this screen)

VOICE_ASSISTANT_INTRO_SCREEN — FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS mix by session position (% of turns on this screen)

PAYMENT_SELECTION — FIND / DECIDE / OTHERS mix by session position (% of turns on this screen)
What changed: Still the noisiest screen, improving slowly: OTHERS 43.2% → 40.5%, DECIDE 42.7% → 43.2%. Power users start ~5pts cleaner than the overall population here but plateau — payment-screen confusion seems structural, not a learning-curve issue.
