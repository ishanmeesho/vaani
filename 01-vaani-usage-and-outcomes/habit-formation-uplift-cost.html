Habit Formation, Uplift & Cost — Vaani 1+OD
Vaani · Pooled 1+OD Test vs Control

Habit formation, order uplift, and whether the cost is worth it

Every Test-arm user in the pooled 1+OD experiments (19179 / 19446 / 20009), bucketed by lifetime Vaani-session count — 0 (never used), 1, 2, 3, 4, or 5+ (habit-formed) — with a Control-arm baseline alongside. The central question: once you control for how often someone simply opens the app, how much of the "habit-formed users order more" story survives, and does the extra Vaani usage that produces it look cheap or expensive per incremental order?

Population: pooled 1+OD Test arm (5,065,273 users) vs Control arm, Vaani-leakage users (any bk_turns_json signal) stripped from Control per standing rule. Window: 2026-05-20 → 2026-07-19 (full available gold+scrap history). Matched cut: restricted to users with ≥27 total app opens in the window — the 5+ bucket's own 25th percentile — so every bucket is compared among users who are, at minimum, as frequent an app-opener as a typical habit-formed user. This doesn't fully equalize frequency (the 5+ bucket still averages more opens even above the floor — see caveats) but removes the bulk of the "they just don't open the app much" confound. Orders / clicks / turns are scalar counts from each session's JSON envelope (orders_json.count, clicks_json.count, bk_turns_json.count). Zero-click % = share of app-open sessions with no click. Sscat/app-open = distinct sub-sub-categories touched via cart_json/orders_json, per app open.

6. Intensity cut — heavy relative Vaani reliance isn't the same as habit formation

Within matched Vaani-users, split at the median of (Vaani sessions ÷ total app opens). Counter-intuitively, the high-ratio half — who lean on Vaani far more per visit — actually order less, not more, despite spending 7x more turns. High ratio looks less like "loves Vaani" and more like "needed more help per visit," plausibly a lower-intent or less-confident shopper profile.

7. Appendix — an even tighter homogeneity check (added 2026-07-23)

Everything above (§2–6) matches on a floor — ≥27 app opens — which still leaves a residual gradient: even above the floor, the 5+ bucket averages 78.5 app opens vs bucket-0's 57.6 (flagged in the caveats). Two ways to tighten this were considered: (a) cap the observation period so every user's app-open count is measured over the same fixed calendar window rather than the full ~61-day span, or (b) stratify into narrow app-open bands so every cohort being compared has a near-identical average app-open count by construction, not just a shared floor. This section runs (b) — it's the more tractable of the two given how this data has to be queried (a calendar-capped rebuild would need a per-user "days since first seen" anchor point not currently computed), and it directly answers "make the averages the same across cohorts." (a) remains a good next refinement, not run here.

Method: every matched user (Test and Control, ≥27 app opens) is placed into one of six app-open bands (27–39, 40–59, 60–79, 80–119, 120–199, 200+). Within each band, Control and every Vaani-session bucket now share an average app-open count that differs by less than 1 app open in every band — a much tighter match than the floor alone produced. Order uplift is then computed within each band and rolled up into a single weighted figure per bucket.

Tightening the match shrinks the apparent uplift further — the 5+ bucket's incremental-order gap vs Test-0 drops from +0.915 (floor-only) to +0.409 (stratified), and turns-per-incremental-order rises from 56.1 to 125.6. Vs Control, cost rises from 59.9 to 185.0 turns/order. This doesn't mean Vaani has no effect — a positive gap survives in every single band, including the tightest ones — but it confirms the floor-only match in §2–5 was still overstating the effect, and the true current cost per incremental order is higher than that section's headline number.

8. Appendix — is shopping effort actually reducing? (added 2026-07-28)

Same matched population (≥27 app opens), asking a different question: as Vaani usage deepens, does it take less browsing to get to a purchase? Three proxies, chosen for what's actually queryable — clicks_json carries product_id but no category, while cart_json/orders_json carry both, so "products seen" is operationalized two ways rather than one: (a) clicks per app‑open (browsing volume, category‑agnostic, from the cheap clicks_json.count field — a true distinct‑products‑clicked count was attempted but the full unnest timed out at population scale, so total click volume is the substitute here), (b) distinct cart‑added products within the sscat(s) the user ultimately ordered from (from cart_json, restricted to order‑placers — the closest available proxy for "how many options did they consider in the category they bought from"), and (c) app‑opens to first order (order‑placers only). All are matched on app‑open frequency exactly as above.

Window note: scrap.va_user_session_activity is currently broken table‑wide ("Metadata not found in transaction log" — a new fault, distinct from the earlier void‑break) as of 2026‑07‑28, so this section rebuilds the matched cohort from gold only (2026‑06‑10 → 2026‑07‑26) rather than the fuller 2026‑05‑20‑onward window used in §1‑7. Bucket sizes here are therefore not directly comparable to the earlier sections' counts (e.g. the 5+ bucket is 61,488 users here vs 51,051 there) — the ≥27‑opens floor and bucket logic are otherwise identical.

The honest answer is no — effort is not reducing. Clicks/app-open rises from 3.60 (never-users) to 4.13 (5+); clicks/order rises from 92.4 to 100.8; distinct cart products considered within the ordered category rises from 4.10 to 5.66 per user (1.21 to 1.33 per order); and app-opens needed to reach a first order rises slightly too, from 16.8 to 19.0 — the 5+ bucket takes marginally more visits to convert than never-users or Control (16.96), not fewer. Every metric points the same direction: heavier Vaani users browse and click somewhat more, not less, and don't reach their first order any faster. This doesn't contradict §2–7's order-uplift finding (they still end up ordering more overall) — but it does mean the mechanism isn't "Vaani gets people to what they want faster." It looks more like heavier Vaani users are simply more thorough/exploratory shoppers across the board, in orders and browsing alike, than like Vaani compressing the path to purchase.

Caveats

  • Correlational, not causal. Users who go on to use Vaani 5+ times may already have more shopping intent than those who don't — matching on app-open frequency narrows one confound, not all of them.
  • The app-open match is a floor, not a band. Even above 27 opens, the 5+ bucket still averages 78.5 opens vs bucket-0's 57.6 — a residual frequency gradient remains inside the "matched" numbers, so the true Vaani-specific uplift is likely smaller than even the matched 1.4x figure suggests.
  • Cost is a relative proxy, not a dollarized figure. Turns per incremental order lets you compare buckets against each other, not against a real infra budget — apply your own ₹/turn figure to the raw turn counts in the data table if you have one.
  • Test-0 as a baseline carries self-selection risk — these are users who saw the same nudges and chose not to engage, which may correlate with lower intent independent of Vaani itself. The Control comparison avoids this but reintroduces ordinary cross-arm randomization noise.
  • Scope: pooled 1+OD experiments only (19179/19446/20009) — does not include experiment 17675, which was added separately to the earlier city-level cohort work in this conversation.
  • §8 uses a different window than §1–7 (2026-06-10 → 07-26, gold-only) because scrap.va_user_session_activity was broken table-wide at query time — bucket populations there are not directly comparable to the earlier sections' counts.
