User-ask repeatability — diminishing curves

Each line is one L1::L2 user-ask tag. The x-axis is the number of turns of that ask a user produced (across all their sessions in the data). The y-axis is % of the users of that ask who produced ≥ N turns, so every line starts at 100% at N=1 and decays from there. Hover a point to see the underlying user count.

FIND · 285,947 users across 4 tag(s)

FIND is a browsing behavior — high engagement, category-driven. The two dominant asks — category_only (106k users) and category_with_attributes (98k users) — are the stickiest FIND shapes: users produce an average of ~5.1 turns apiece, and roughly 1 in 4 users (23-24%) keep asking 6+ times. These are shoppers who use Vaani to keep browsing within a category rather than one-shot searchers. In contrast, attributes_only and no_category asks decay much faster — about half of those users ask only once (~50% single-turn) and the mean drops to ~3 turns. Reads as: when the user gives Vaani a clear category, they engage repeatedly; when the ask is vague, they typically don't come back to it.

DECIDE · 148,951 users across 8 tag(s)

DECIDE::price completely dominates the branch — the rest is one-and-done Q&A. price is by far the stickiest ask in the entire dataset: 8.7 turns / user on average, 35% of users produce 6+ turns, and only 32% ask a single time. Users clearly hammer on price (negotiation attempts, discount checks, re-asks after seeing new products). Every other DECIDE ask — quality, delivery_time, size_fit, quantity_pack_contents, how_to_use, other_product_information — looks like a fact-lookup pattern: 1.7–2.9 turns / user mean, with 55-70% asking exactly once. comparison is essentially not used (482 users, 1.15 mean — one-shot).

OTHERS · 104,495 users across 3 tag(s)

OTHERS is noise — modest repeat, majority single-turn. All three sub-tags (unresolved, off_journey, action_led_ask) share the same profile: 2.1–2.7 turns / user mean and 54-62% single-turn users. There's a small long tail (a handful of users go 20+ turns) but the modal user asks once and moves on. Since these asks fall outside the outcome tree, they don't have a "good/bad" grade, so the only signal here is volume + repeat shape.
