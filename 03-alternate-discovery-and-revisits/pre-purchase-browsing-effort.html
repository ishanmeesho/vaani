Insight The pattern is directionally identical across every gender cut — nowhere does single-sscat browsing out-convert multi-sscat browsing. Practical read This does not prove clicking more categories causes a purchase — it's equally consistent with intent-to-buy driving both behaviours (a shopper already planning to spend more browses more broadly). Either way, the operational implication holds: friction that stops a user moving fluidly between related sub-categories (slow category switches, feed resets, cart loss on category change) is plausibly costing conversions, since it is precisely the multi-category sessions that convert best. It also reframes P1's "wandering" finding — a session that clicks into a different category before buying is not automatically confused; broader exploration correlates with a higher chance of buying, not a lower one.

Putting it together — what's actually happening

Four independently-built queries, on different session universes, land on the same story. P1 (all 47.7M buying sessions, corrected — see fix note above) shows buyers click roughly 8.65 distinct products per purchase, and just over half of those (51%) are the exact sscat they buy, rising to 75% at category grain — comparison shopping across close alternatives explains most of the rest, with genuine cross-category wandering (25.5%) real but a minority behaviour. P2 (all 693.5M sessions, bought or not) shows that wandering is not a warning sign: sessions that touch 2+ sub-categories convert at 2.5x the rate of single-sscat sessions. P2b (the 28.2M buyers from those 2+ sscat sessions) shows that lift doesn't mean they simply confirm a favourite — 36% of them end up buying a sscat other than the one they clicked most, versus just 0.32% "leakage" when there was only one sscat to begin with. P3 (the 3.65M sessions where a buyer's path to their eventual purchase could be traced, corrected — see fix note above) shows that even after a buyer has already clicked the exact thing they'll buy, 28% of what they click next is still a different sscat, and 14% is a different category entirely — the wandering doesn't stop once the right item is found, it continues right up to checkout, just less than the uncorrected numbers suggested.

The portfolio pattern is the clearest through-line: Women Sarees, Kurtis & Kurtas, Top Wear and Jewellery show up as high-effort, high-focus, low-post-anchor-wandering in both P1 and P3 — buyers in these portfolios do a lot of browsing, but nearly all of it is genuine in-category comparison shopping. Kitchen Utility, Unisex Personal Care, Health & Wellness, Stationery and Baby & Mother Care show up as low-effort, low-focus, high-post-anchor-wandering in both parts — these look like fast, need-driven purchases where a buyer clicks a handful of unrelated things (likely feed/recommendation exposure or incidental browsing) rather than actively comparing alternatives. That two separately-authored queries — one covering all buying sessions, one covering only sessions with a traceable anchor path — agree on this split is a real finding, not a coincidence of one query's design.

What this suggests for Meesho Cross-category and cross-sscat browsing — before and after a buyer has found what they'll eventually buy — is associated with higher conversion, not lower. The operational takeaway from all three parts together: invest in making comparison and cross-navigation fast and frictionless in the high-effort/high-focus fashion portfolios (where users are already doing this manually), and don't over-index on "reducing wandering" as a goal for the low-effort/high-wandering utility portfolios, since that wandering looks incidental to fast, already-decided purchases rather than a sign of a broken discovery experience.

Methodology & caveats

Population. P1 covers the 47,661,782 sessions in the window that resulted in at least one order, grouped by (session, sscat/scat/category/portfolio bought, user) — a session that bought across two portfolios is counted once per portfolio purchased. Unique-product counts are computed per session first, then summed up to the portfolio/gender/date grain — the original version applied COUNT(DISTINCT product_id) directly at the rolled-up grain, which collapsed repeat clicks on the same popular product across different sessions into a single count; see the correction banner at the top of the report. P2 covers every session with a resolvable product click in the window, regardless of purchase outcome, split by count of distinct sscat_id values clicked (1 vs. 2+). P2b restricts P2's population to the 44.18M sessions that converted, and for each compares the bought product's sscat (joined from order_master_bi via product_id) against that session's "dominant" sscat — whichever sscat_id received the most clicks in-session, ties broken arbitrarily by lowest sscat_id. P3 covers the 3,647,299 sessions (7–9 Aug) where the bought sscat/scat/category was clicked before purchase, so an anchor point exists; its unique-product counts use the same session-scoped COUNT(DISTINCT session_id || product_id) fix as P1, applied after the same grain bug was found in P3's original query — see the correction banner above the P3 section.

Effort measurement. "Clicks" = product-card opens (silver.mixpanel_android__catalog_opened_main), not scroll impressions or search-result exposure — a product scrolled past without opening is not counted. Category grain is resolved by joining product_id to a taxonomy snapshot (platinum.product_supplier_catalog_attributes, latest dim__dt) that carries sscat_id → scat_id → category_id.

Anchor & post-anchor window (P3). The anchor is the earliest click (at or before the first order) matching the bought sscat/scat/category, computed independently at each grain. The post-anchor window is every click from that anchor through the purchase, split into "same" (matches the anchor's grain) vs. "different."

Windowing. P1/P2 use the last 5 fully completed days as of the analysis date (6–10 Aug 2026); P3 covers 7–9 Aug only (3 days, following its own re-run). All portfolios combined. Portfolio cuts in P1b are restricted to the 43 (of 67) portfolios with >200,000 buying sessions in-window, to avoid long-tail noise.

Population (P4). For every session that bought a product on 8 Aug 2026, P4 finds every other product clicked in the same sscat before that purchase (excluding the bought product itself), then compares bought vs. clicked on price (catalog min/max midpoint), all-history average rating and review-image presence (from order_master_bi, all dates), and 10 finalized taxonomy attributes parsed from product_supplier_catalog_attributes' taxonomy JSON (latest snapshot). Rows are rolled up to (portfolio × all 12 comparison flags), summing unique_clicks (distinct session+clicked-product pairs) and total_clicks (raw click events) — click-repeat-count is aggregated, not used as a grouping key, to avoid the combinatorial row-count blow-up a near-continuous integer causes when grouped alongside 12 categorical flags. All P4 percentages in this report use unique_clicks as the weight.
