Vaani for 1OD+

Based on an experiment in which users continued to engage with Vaani beyond the 1OD stage, we concluded that Vaani has a proven user-level conversion signal (at 1OD stage) but is constrained by adoption. Trial usage is real, habit formation is weak, and the current intervention model is too passive. Details are here.

Executive summary

Learning leap / unknownConverted to knownProgress / update
LL1 — Can Vaani solve passive exploration sessions by intervening at the right moment and creating aha moments that compound into habit? No Based on experiment data: 1. Trial is strong, habit is weak: 14.6% of Test 1OD users use Vaani at least once, but only 1.55% of sessions contain it. 2. Among 1OD adopters, engagement drops off quickly: 53.6% use Vaani only once, 17.7% return for a second use, and just 11.8% use it more than five times. Overall, ~40.9% of users return, while ~50.9% discontinue usage despite opening at least three apps after their last Vaani interaction. 3. Zero-click / passive exploration is the clearest first space: users view products but do not click or show confusion. The sharpest cohort is 0-1 non-text-searchers (+9.0% orders/user, +6.0% clicks/user vs Control). 4. Order rate moves from ~1.9% pre-Vaani to 6.0% in the Vaani session and category exploration from ~0.9 to 2.4 categories/session for same user. Next Steps: 1. Launched Vaani on the platform-homogenized base as the next step to understand queries/usage by mature users. 2. We will launch an experiment to test proactive context rich engagement in viewed-no-click /no-interaction sessions. Success = move passive exploration to a meaningful click or interaction.
LL2 — Once a user engages, can Vaani translate intent into the right results? No 1. Explore and Exploit are ~95% of 1OD usage (Explore 52.7%, Exploit 41.1%, Decision-to-buy 3.6%). 2. Vaani sessions are more exploratory — same-user comparison shows 2.44 SSCATs/session vs 1.15 in their own non-Vaani sessions. 3. Filtering is also strong use case: price/budget (24.7%), negative preference (19.3%), size/fit (18.0%), brand/model (15.1%), recipient/user (13.2%). In 1OD, size/fit sessions order at 12.8% vs 9.6% for no-filter sessions. Next Steps: We will prioritize this after sizing the different use cases across user segments based on the results of the above experiment. We will solve post-adoption experience through conversation design, visual result presentation, seamless app transitions, and clear next-step guidance.
Overall decision from discussionTwo problems to solve: (1) Adoption — intervene at the right moment in zero-click / passive exploration journeys to create the first aha moment; (2) Post-adoption experience — solve intent → resolution through conversation design, visual presentation and app changes so Vaani becomes a repeat part of the shopping journey.

LoDs and CPDs

LoD / CPD themeProblem discoveredData InsightsNext steps
Overall impact shape Vaani is moving user-level conversion, but not yet moving every-session conversion strongly. Test conversion is 41.61% vs Control 40.64%, a +0.98 pp / +2.4% lift, statistically significant. Session order rate is only 3.66% vs 3.63%, +0.03 pp / +0.9%, not significant. Fixed-window lift is positive across 1d, 3d, 7d, 14d, 28d, with the strongest read at 7 days: 32.16% vs 30.48%, +1.67 pp / +5.5%. Track two separate goals: adoption / penetration and post-adoption experience quality.
Zero-click / no-search adoption cohort The clearest adoption opportunity is users who are browsing but not actively channelizing intent. 0-1 non-text-searcher segment shows +1.34 pp conversion, +9.0% orders/user, +6.0% clicks/user. (in their 1OD → next order stage) Make zero-click / viewed-but-did-not-click / high-dwell-no-click the first intervention cohort. Success metric should be first meaningful click after Vaani prompt, not only order.
Carry-forward failure Vaani usage in 0-1 is not carrying forward into 1OD. Among Test 1OD users who used Vaani in 0-1, only 38.0% used Vaani again in 1OD; 62.0% did not.
Vaani is episodic, not default Even users who eventually use Vaani do not use it in most 1OD sessions. User-level Vaani reach rises in later app-open buckets: 4.8% on app-open 1, 7.7% in app-opens 11–20, 9.1% in app-opens 21–50. But session share falls/stays low: 4.8% on app-open 1, 1.8% in 11–20, 1.4% in 21–50, 1.1% in 51–100 and 101+. Do not force Vaani into every session. Build trigger logic for moments where the user is likely stuck: zero-click, repeated PDP exits, high refine behavior, duplicate clicks, or text fallback.
Lifecycle conversion gap Vaani sessions remain much higher-intent than non-Vaani sessions across the lifecycle. Vaani vs non-Vaani order rate by app-open bucket: app-open 1 21.3% vs 9.0%, app-open 6–10 10.4% vs 4.0%, app-open 21–50 9.8% vs 3.4%, app-open 101+ 7.3% vs 2.6%. Use these high-intent patterns to design proactive triggers. Compare triggered users against matched non-triggered users, not only raw Vaani vs non-Vaani sessions.
Explore is largest use case Find-for-me / discovery is the largest Vaani job in 1OD. 1OD Vaani theme split: Explore 52.7%, Exploit 41.1%, Decision-to-buy 3.6%. Assisted discovery alone is 32.0% of 1OD sessions. Occasion/gift/recipient discovery is 13.4% and has 12.4% order rate. Prioritize Find for me as the first experience bet: vague need → clarifying question → shortlist → visible reason for recommendations.
Explore broadens category discovery Vaani materially increases category exploration, not just clicks. In same-user comparison, Vaani sessions show 2.44 SSCATs/session vs 1.15 in non-Vaani sessions. Event-study: pre-Vaani sessions have ~0.9 categories/session, first Vaani jumps to 2.4, and post-Vaani ordinary sessions settle at ~1.2 categories/session, +30% vs pre. Track new SSCAT clicked after Vaani, new category order, and N2C confidence metrics as Explore success measures.
Exploit / narrowing is high-volume Users often come with constraints, but the app needs to translate those constraints into visible filters and better results. 1OD Exploit is 41.1% of Vaani sessions with 10.9% order rate, 92.9% click rate, 9.62 clicks/session, and 3.58 SSCATs/session. Single-constraint search is 21.7% of 1OD sessions; multi-constraint narrowing is 19.4%. Intent Channelisation to be picked later
Single vs multi-constraint behavior Multi-constraint journeys are deeper, but single-constraint journeys convert well and may be easier to scale. In 1OD, single-constraint sessions have 2.93 avg turns, 91.7% click rate, 11.0% order rate, 8.66 clicks/session. Multi-constraint sessions have 5.94 avg turns, 94.4% click rate, 10.7% order rate, 10.69 clicks/session. Intent Channelisation to be picked later
High-yield filters Some filters convert better and should be prioritized in prompts. 1OD filter order rates: delivery urgency 13.7%, size/fit 12.8%, material/fabric 12.5%, recipient/user 12.4%, negative preference 11.7%, brand/model 11.6%, vs no explicit filter 9.6%. Material/fabric has 15.20 clicks/session; quantity/combo has 15.26 clicks/session. Intent Channelisation to be picked later
Discontinuation quality buckets Most discontinuation is “explored but did not become habit,” not hard failure. Discontinued bucket split: 54.3% clicked/explored but no order, 18.0% clicked + text-search fallback, 11.7% clicked + dissatisfaction/refinement, 7.4% no downstream click, 7.4% converted in session, 0.7% no click + fallback, 0.6% high-turn no-click. Treat buckets differently: zero-click = result quality issue; fallback = handoff/query issue; explored-no-order = continuation and memory issue; converted = post-purchase reactivation issue.
