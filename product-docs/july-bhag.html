H2 update

Executive Summary

Key Unknown Major Takeaways
Are users adopting Vaani, and is deeper usage worth chasing? YES. 7.7% of Test users adopt Vaani. But only 6.5% adopters reach 5 session+ Holding exposure constant (same runway of app opens since first use), users who reach 5+ Vaani sessions place ~35% more orders and click ~43% more products per session than single-session users, with a lower zero-click rate (27.4% vs 34.9%).
What separates users who come back from those who don't? Engagement depth in the very first conversation turns (9.1 vs 2.4), searches (16.7 vs 5.9), more screens touched, lesser session time, not purchase intent (flat), not technical friction, not adoption channel. A search-led first session bounces far less than a non-search one (41.7% vs 60.2%).
What are users actually using Vaani for? Overwhelmingly to (FIND). Find is 70.7% of turns on the search page and 63.7% on the homepage; on PDP, 1 in 4 turns redirects to a new search. Fresh search is also the single most self-reinforcing behavior on every surface (49–74% self-repeat) users who return on Vaani come back to search again.
Are outcomes good on that search behavior? Where is the constraint? No — Find converts worst everywhere: session order rate 8.2–8.9% vs Decide's 9.7 -- 13.2%; on PDP, Find sessions order the product discussed at just 1.2% vs 8.5% for Decide. On the search page, 30% of turns where Vaani took no action were real Find intents it failed to act on. Users keep searching but we aren't showing the right results. Improving search outcomes (refine / switch / resume) is the constraint to break next.

Learning Leap 2

Are session-level outcomes improving for repeat users, and what will we solve to improve them? (Focus: Vaani is used primarily used for FIND so improving search outcomes on the three surfaces where it happens [product page / search page / homepage] is where the leverage sits)

Learning Milestones / KRsStatusSuccess metricTargetCurrentKey Updates / DetailsNext steps (8 weeks)Key reflections (max 3 per leap)
M1 · Establish what users use Vaani for and what session outcomes they get DONE FIND / DECIDE / Other share of turns per surface; self-repeat rates; session conversion by dominant behavior type - Vaani is overwhelmingly a search alternative: Find is 70.7% of search-page turns 63.7% of homepage turns; on PDP, 1 in 4 turns redirects to a new search. Fresh search is the most self-reinforcing behavior on every surface (49–74% self-repeat) — returning users come back to search again. But search outcomes are the weakest: FIND sessions convert worst everywhere — order rate 8.2% (search page) / 8.9% (homepage) vs Decide's 9.7–13.2%; on PDP, FIND sessions order the product discussed at 1.2% vs 8.5% for Decide. On the search page, 30% of no-action turns were real Find intents Vaani failed to act on. Freeze “improve search outcomes” as the LL2 improvement surface. 1. Repeat usage ≠ good outcomes users come back to search again, but search is where we convert worst. 2. Users don't “graduate” to higher-intent Decide behavior with experience; the mix is structural per surface. 3. So this is a results-quality problem, not an adoption problem. We aren't showing the right results.
M2 · Locate the leverage which query types, on which surfaces DONE Sizing of refine vs fresh vs resume intents per surface; missed-intent rate Pick the biggest volume × biggest outcome gap behaviors Search page (738K turns): refine-type queries (similar / budget / cheaper / better quality) are 44% of search-action turns; 30% of no-action turns are missed Find intents. PDP (1.04M turns): 25% fire a new search, of which 42% are “same thing, different attribute” refinements. Homepage (292K turns): 57.5% fresh search where users start and re-start intents. Three focus behaviors chosen: (1) user refines an intent from the product page, (2) user refines an intent on the search page, (3) user resumes or opens an intent on the homepage. Intent switches get a separate, lighter handling: help the user save current products for later, or capture what they didn't like. Build the interventions per surface (M3). 1. When Vaani fires a search it's almost always a real FIND intent (91.7%) the gap is in what we show next, plus the 30% of silent turns where we do not search are also FIND intent – here we are failing to act. 2. Refinement queries carry an explicit user constraint that we currently aren’t able to utilise best because our default mode is to rely on search only.
M3 · Improve FIND outcomes on the three focus behaviors (refine on PDP, refine on search page, resume on homepage) PLANNED Session order rate and zero-click rate on refine / resume sessions PDP refine: open a results surface that keeps the anchor product's context while applying the stated constraint. Search-page refine: voice out the right filters for the query and auto-apply them; for each search, maintain the next set of important questions to ask — questions that perform well automatically surface higher. Homepage resume: proactively offer to continue the user's open intent at app-open; feed answers back into the feed so results visibly improve. Design conversation flow, UI and results per surface; TBD
M4 · Information enrichment — ground Vaani's answers in real product, user & region data PLANNED Order rate on enriched turns (primary focus: DECIDE turns); % of turns where an enriched field is surfaced vs suppressed Vaani today answers in generic language because it lacks grounded per-user / per-product / per-region signal. 33 fields enumerated across 8 clusters (seller & product trust, social proof, visual truth, personalization, commercial context, order state, alternates, memory), priority-tiered P0 (21) / P1 (10) / P2 (2). Every field is defined as field × shape: what it is, all shapes the LLM can consume it in (absolute / comparative / trend / reassurance / warning), when to surface it, and when to stay silent — thin samples return null, not noise. Highest-impact examples: return rate vs category norm, return-reason mix ("mostly size, not quality"), size reco from the user's own order + return history, price ladder & sale calendar, delivery ETA, in-session memory ("wo laal wali"). 1. Better answers need better inputs — most of Vaani's generic responses trace to missing context, not model quality. 2. Silent-when rules matter as much as surfacing rules — a field with no suppression threshold is noise in the context window.
