First-ask outcome → same-ask repeat probability

For each L1::L2 user ask: we take each user's first turn of that ask on or after 2026-06-17 (Vaani experiment start). We grade that first turn's outcome as good/bad using the Tier-2 rules (rules 1–4 you gave; B1b_* and _sscat_unknown counted as bad). We then check whether the same user produced another turn of the same ask later in the dataset. Cohorts with a good first outcome and cohorts with a bad first outcome are compared side-by-side. Bars: repeat probability. Hover for underlying user counts. na-first users (outcome couldn't be graded — OTHERS L1 or clicks-void window) are excluded from the good/bad comparison but shown in the table.

FIND

For FIND, the "did the first turn succeed?" signal is weak — and inverted for vague asks. Category-driven FIND asks (category_only, category_with_attributes) show a modest lift: a good first turn nudges repeat rate from ~58% → ~62% and adds ~0.25 turns to the mean (lift ≈ 1.06–1.08x). Small effect, but directionally right.

But attributes_only and no_category actually invert (0.89–0.98x) — users whose ambiguous first ask went badly come back and ask the same thing more, not less. Reads as: bad-first users are still searching for what they wanted; good-first users got resolved and moved on. FIND repeat is mostly a shopper-intent signal, not a Vaani-quality signal.

DECIDE

DECIDE is where a good first turn actually matters — clear, large, and consistent. Every DECIDE ask (except tiny comparison) shows a meaningful uplift on both axes when the first turn was good: repeat rate lifts 1.14–1.37x and average total turns lift 1.10–1.30x. The standout is DECIDE::price: after a good first turn, 84% of users repeat (vs 63% after a bad first) and they produce 10.7 turns on average (vs 8.2). delivery_time shows the highest repeat-% lift (1.37x), quantity_pack_contents the strongest turns lift after price.

Business read: making the first DECIDE answer useful (correct, specific, in-context) is worth optimizing — it directly deepens engagement on the ask that already dominates the DECIDE branch.

OTHERS

OTHERS turns aren't gradable, so no cohort comparison is possible. All OTHERS asks (unresolved, off_journey, action_led_ask) fall outside the outcome tree and end up in not_applicable_L1_others. Every user's first OTHERS turn is graded na, so we can't split good-first vs bad-first cohorts. The bars will therefore be empty here — this section is included only for completeness and to surface the ~103k na-first users counted for OTHERS tags.
