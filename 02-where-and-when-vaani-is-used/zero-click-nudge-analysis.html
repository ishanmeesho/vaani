Vaani · Experiment 20958

Zero-Click Nudges: reach, adoption & effect

Control 168647 · Conservative Nudge 168648 · Aggressive Nudge 168649 — three-arm test of nudging users toward the Vaani voice assistant when a session risks ending without a product click.

Window: 2026-07-17 → 2026-07-25 (9 days) Source: gold.va_user_session_activity Corrected: 2026-07-31
Major correction, 2026-07-31 — read this first. Every prior version of this report defined "adopted" using the nudge's own adopted flag (a mic-tap within 60 seconds of the nudge). That flag proves the user tapped the mic — it does not prove Vaani actually produced a response. Checked directly: of every zero-click nudge event flagged adopted=true, only 35.6% have a real bk_turns_json conversation turn within 60 seconds of the nudge. The rest are taps that didn't lead anywhere — a UI open, an error, an abandoned mic. "Adopted" now means: mic tap AND a real Vaani turn within 60 seconds of the nudge. This tightens every adoption-rate number in this report substantially (family/rule adopt rates roughly halve), and reshapes the qualitative dump's population (96,558 → 84,824 sessions — the 12,734 that don't survive were mic-taps with no timely turn). Every section below has been rebuilt against this definition. Where a number moved, the old value is shown alongside for transparency.

Conversion improvement across cohorts — both experiments, tenure-matched

Refreshed to the most current data (window 2026-07-17–28, 12 days — gold's rolling range and experiment tagging both advanced since the last pull). Four cohorts for 20958: Control (leakage-excluded) · non-Vaani (Test-arm, never used Vaani) · Vaani, non-zero-click adopter (used Vaani via organic use or "other" nudges, never strictly adopted a zero-click nudge) · Vaani, zero-click adopter (strict adoption: mic-tap + real turn within 60s). Exp 19446 has no zero-click nudges at all, so it gets three cohorts instead of four — otherwise identical design. Tenure-matched: 19446 has been running since 2026-06-17, so its cohorts here are restricted to users who first entered the 19446 audience during this same 07-17–28 window — i.e. genuinely new to the experiment, the same tenure as every 20958 user by construction.

The headline finding, in both experiments: Vaani users convert meaningfully better than everyone else — including Control. Session order rate for Vaani users is +10–12% relative to Control in 20958 (3.66–3.82% vs 3.42%) and +16.5% in 19446 (4.67% vs 4.01%). Meanwhile, Test-arm users who never engage Vaani at all convert worse than Control in both experiments (−5 to −6.5%) — being merely eligible for Vaani nudges without acting on them doesn't help, and this population likely skews toward lower-intent browsers to begin with (a selection effect, not evidence the experiment itself hurts non-adopters).

Zero-click adopters land in between: their zero-click session rate (31.2–31.5%) sits above Vaani-non-ZC users (30.5–30.7%, essentially matching Control) but below non-Vaani users (32.1–32.3%) — consistent with the whole report's story: these are users who needed the nudge (higher baseline browsing-without-clicking risk), and adopting it pulls them back most of the way to normal, without fully erasing the gap. Their sessions are also strikingly shorter (207s vs. 399–507s for Vaani-non-ZC users) and need more clicks per order (99.4–99.7 vs. 96.6–97.1) — a distinct behavioral signature: quick, transactional "get me to the answer" sessions, vs. Vaani-non-ZC users' longer, more exploratory browsing-plus-chatting sessions.

20958's zero-click nudges correlate with meaningfully deeper repeat usage: only 60–61% of its adopters stop at 1 session, vs. 68% for 19446 — despite 19446's Vaani users having a slightly higher raw session-adoption rate. The consistent exception is Test-arm users who never engage Vaani at all, who underperform Control — a reminder that these comparisons are correlational: users self-select into Vaani engagement, and some of what looks like a Vaani effect may reflect who chooses to engage rather than what engaging does to them.

Methodology & caveats

  • Strict adoption, precisely defined: a nudge event counts as adopted only if nudges_json.adopted='true' AND there exists a bk_turns_json event in the same session with ts between the nudge's ts and 60 seconds later. Computed via date_diff('second', nudge_ts, turn_ts) between 0 and 60 in Presto.
  • Window: 2026-07-17 to 2026-07-25 (9 days). Cohort sizes drift slightly across queries in this report (e.g. 1,076,282 vs. 1,081,043 in earlier pulls) because gold's rolling window and experiment tagging continued to advance across the multi-day gap while this correction was being built — not a methodology change, just normal data growth.
  • Control leakage: Control-arm users showing a Vaani signal are excluded from any Control-side metric, per standing rule.
  • First-adoption attribution uses day-level ordering from the full gold history with the strict-adoption join applied to the full history too (not just the window) — this is the most expensive query in the rebuild, since it requires unnesting both nudges_json and bk_turns_json across every day a user has both fields populated.
  • Vaani-vs-organic search attribution (qualitative dump) is unchanged from the prior correction: search_results_json tagged Vaani-driven via proximity to vaani_searches_json, then inherited by the nearest following "Search"-real-estate product click.
  • Correlational, not causal, throughout.
